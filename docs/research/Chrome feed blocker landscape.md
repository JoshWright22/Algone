# Existing feed blockers leave reliability unclaimed

Yes, feed-blocking Chrome extensions already exist in large numbers, and for most people the right answer is to install one rather than build one. **News Feed Eradicator (NFE) is the multi-site default at 200,000 users and 4.5 stars, free and open source; Unhook leads YouTube at 1,000,000 users and 4.9 stars, also free but closed source.** Manifest V3 no longer separates anyone: all 427 live listings harvested for this research are MV3, so update recency is the useful quality signal. Hiding itself is priced at $0 on nearly every site, and paid tiers sell extras such as schedules, sync, and filters. Complaints are a small minority (about 3% low-star ratings for NFE on Chrome) but they concentrate on one thing: blockers silently break when sites change, and fixes reach users slowly. NFE's August and October 2026 fixes for Facebook and LinkedIn were still not in its store build on October 31. A new extension is justified only as a reliability and precision product across a few painful sites, led by LinkedIn and Facebook, with a fast selector-update path. Building for yourself alone is justified only if uBlock Origin Lite filters and the free tools fall short. The evidence supports a modest opening, not widespread dissatisfaction, and it excludes Reddit, which could not be accessed.

## Dozens of tools exist, but only about ten multi-site ones exceed 1,000 users

The field is crowded and steeply top-heavy. A store sweep of about 40 searches found roughly 90 multi-site candidates, yet **only about ten have 1,000 or more users**, and most 2025-2026 entrants have under 100. Newer tools have also drifted from removing the home feed toward blocking Shorts, Reels, and Explore. Single-site tools split into three types that are easy to confuse: true feed removers (Unhook, Antigram, LinkOff), filters that keep a feed (Control Panel for Twitter, F.B. Purity), and rule-based blockers (BlockTube, YTBlock). Only the first type competes directly on hiding the feed.

"None listed" does not prove a tool is free, and "no repo found" is a weak negative rather than confirmed closed source. Unhook's rating is notably higher on Chrome than on [Firefox, at 4.63 from 1,199 ratings](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/addons/addon/youtube-recommended-videos/). Most 5.0 scores on small tools rest on fewer than 15 ratings and say little. Whole-site blockers are adjacent competitors that users often pair with feed hiders: [BlockSite](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh) (1,000,000 users), [StayFocusd](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji) (700,000), and [LeechBlock NG](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/blaaajhemilngeeffpbfkdjjoefldkok) (100,000, 4.9 stars).

Coverage by site is uneven. YouTube is saturated, with at least eight listings at 30,000 or more users. X is strong but concentrated in one filter-style project, and whole-feed removal there sits in tiny tools such as [X (Twitter) Cleaner](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/nejdcgfebfamlgpggncmbpkcnefaiemk) (279 users). Single-site options are weak for Facebook, where the tool explicitly hiding the home feed has [159 users](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/amlhflamnmllpgpcpicpbcfpoggidfjn), and for TikTok. Multi-site tools narrow these holes, since NFE covers Facebook and X, and Escape the Algorithm and SocialFocus cover TikTok, so single-site counts alone overstate the opening.

## MV3 is settled, so update recency is the real health check

Chrome stopped running MV2 extensions from [Chrome 139, with store removal set for August 31, 2026](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/mv2-deprecation-timeline), and [third-party reports confirm the removal happened](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/9/google-has-removed-manifest-v2-extensions-from-chrome-web-store-including-ublock-origin/). **All 427 retrievable listings in the harvest declared MV3**, including tools untouched since 2021 and 2022 such as [Focus](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mapbhjcejbjaldfnhbcffdjifefhnjak). MV3 therefore proves only that a package is installable, not that its selectors still match live pages.

Casualties do exist. Nine known items no longer resolve, including the [original DF Tube](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mjdepdfccjgcndkmemponafgioodelna), which an undated [cached listing](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/df-tube-distraction-free/mjdepdfccjgcndkmemponafgioodelna?hl=en-US) showed at about 400,000 users, along with three LinkedIn blockers and [TikTok Eradicator](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/dfenmdjldedloklnjjkdaaomifnggiio). The store gives no reasons, so the MV2 purge is a likely cause for some of these rather than an established one.

Maintenance is better judged by the gap between code and store. Unhook's last Chrome build dates to March 2026, a seven-month gap for a tool tied to YouTube's markup. Antigram's [repo has commits through October 2026](https://github.com/aymyo/antigram-extension), but its store build is from May 2025. NFE's maintainer is active, yet says he [mostly considers the project "done"](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator) and takes pull requests mainly for bug fixes. **NFE's [store build is 3.0.5 from July 16](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fjcldmjmjhkklehbacihaiopjklihlgg), while Facebook fixes from August 20 and a LinkedIn fix from [October 28](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/commits/main/src/sitelist) sit unreleased in the repo**, as [package.json remains at 3.0.5](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/blob/main/package.json). That puts the oldest of those fixes more than 70 days from users. This is the market leader's clearest weakness.

MV3's constraints barely touch cosmetic hiding, which needs CSS and content scripts rather than blocking network requests. The constraint that matters is the [ban on remotely hosted code](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/mv3-requirements), because it shapes how quickly a repair can ship.

## Users complain about breakage first, and rarely about money or privacy

Satisfaction is high overall. NFE on Chrome has [50 one-star and 23 two-star ratings out of 2,353](https://extscope.org/extension/fjcldmjmjhkklehbacihaiopjklihlgg), and [77 of Unhook's 1,199 Firefox ratings](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/ratings/rating/?addon=youtube-recommended-videos&score=1,2&page_size=25) are low. Most [Hacker News comments mentioning NFE](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=news%20feed%20eradicator&tags=comment&numericFilters=created_at_i%3E1704067200&hitsPerPage=30) since 2024 are recommendations or workarounds, so "people hate the incumbents" would misstate the market.

**Breakage is the clear top complaint.** NFE has [372 issues in total and 87 open](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues). Its most-commented issue is [LinkedIn's feed not being removed (#362, 39 comments)](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/362), and among its 29 latest issues, 7 involve LinkedIn and 7 involve Facebook. Causes are mundane: [LinkedIn moved home to /feed/foryou/](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/596), and [Reddit changed a URL](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/554). Of the 20 written reviews in a sample of Unhook's low-star Firefox ratings, [16 concerned breakage or perceived abandonment](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/ratings/rating/?addon=youtube-recommended-videos&score=1,2&page_size=25), though that is a Firefox sample of unhappy users and may not reflect Chrome. RYS shows the mechanism well: [one October 2026 pull request repaired six settings at once](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/pull/242), including subscription filters that had quietly done nothing.

Mobile comes second, with [at least five recent NFE reports](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/607) and a [maintainer-owned v3.1 milestone](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/495) listing four separate obstacles. Third is a complaint few would predict: **over-blocking**. NFE has hidden [LinkedIn's post box](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/571), [Instagram Stories](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/544), and [Facebook Groups](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/566), while Unhook reviewers on Firefox report broken playback. Over-blocking comes from the same fragile page-matching as under-blocking, and it damages the task people came to do.

The remaining items are thinner. Flashing is real but sparsely reported ([#588](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/588), [#507](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/507)), and users may not bother to file a brief flash. Bypass pulls both ways: one user [wants more snooze friction](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/546), and another was [locked out by a frozen unlock screen](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/561). Paywalls are not an issue for NFE or Unhook, though [UnTrap's 2.55 rating](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/enboaomnljigfhfjfoalacienlhjlfil) at 100,000 users stands out. Its review text was not analyzed, so no cause can be assigned.

No evidence shows NFE or Unhook being sold, paywalled, or caught tracking, which is an absence of evidence rather than proof. Trust is a category risk: [BlockSite's listing discloses analysis of visited URLs](https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/blocksite/privacy/), and [older sale-then-adware cases](https://www.nbcnews.com/technolog/google-yanks-two-chrome-extensions-invasive-ads-2d11960552) exist outside this niche. Unhook's [publisher is anonymous](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/addons/addon/youtube-recommended-videos/), and two low-star reviews object to its closed source. Lookalikes add confusion, such as [Newsfeed Eradicator 2.0](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/clfonknljamomadgkcciofnhfegjinhd), a separate listing rated 2.4. Separately, [LinkedIn scans for 6,236 extensions](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/linkedin-secretely-scans-for-6-000-plus-chrome-extensions-collects-data), though no source says feed blockers are on that list.

Requests follow the same pattern and engagement is low: the highest reaction count seen was [14, for schedules](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/8). That issue is closed as completed, yet a [reviewer still asked for a schedule in October 2026](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/news-feed-eradicator/fjcldmjmjhkklehbacihaiopjklihlgg/reviews), and whether NFE really ships one is unresolved. Users also [stack NFE with LeechBlock, Cold Turkey, or One Sec](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45090170), which suggests no single tool covers everything, although they may simply be content to stack.

## Only reliability and precision justify a new extension

Most candidate gaps fail on demand, or are already filled. Strength below reflects evidence of the problem, not a measured audience, since no uninstall or churn data exists.

| Gap | Evidence | Already served? | Justifies building? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survives site changes, ships fixes fast | Strong: [repeated LinkedIn outages](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/362), Unhook reviews, NFE's release lag | No tool demonstrates it | **Yes, the core case** |
| Precise hiding that keeps useful features | Moderate: [#540](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/540), [Hacker News on Groups and Marketplace](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47091984) | Partly: [No Distractions](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/looidefpafaogockjglamdijbaocichg) redirects, Hide offers 100+ controls | **Yes, paired with the above** |
| Stories, Reels, Shorts in a free multi-site tool | Moderate: [#509](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/509), [#559](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/559) | Largely: NFE partly, SocialFocus, Hide, Escape | Only as a supporting feature |
| Mobile browsers | Moderate, and possibly the largest audience | [SocialFocus claims mobile sites](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/abocjojdmemdpiffeadpdnicnlhcndcg); NFE planned | No. A Chrome extension likely cannot reach it; verify first |
| Temporary unlock, schedules, and open source together | Weak to moderate | [Escape the Algorithm](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/escape-the-algorithm-soci/ichpbppanfcipepejbhffpcbdghfbggl) has the friction model; [RYS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/cdhdichomdnlaadbndgmagohccgpejae) schedules YouTube only | Not alone, but a cheap differentiator |
| TikTok, Facebook, or whole-X single-site tools | Supply gap only; demand unmeasured | Multi-site tools overlap | Not alone; TikTok and Instagram use is likely mobile-heavy |
| No flash, LLM filtering, progressive friction | Weak: [a few](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/issues/588) [anecdotes](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49408076) | Mostly unserved | No |

The bar differs by audience. **For personal use, do not start by building.** Install NFE or Unhook, or use uBlock Origin Lite, which [gained custom filters and external list subscriptions by June 2026](https://github.com/uBlockOrigin/uBOL-home/wiki/Frequently-asked-questions-(FAQ)), so many older articles are out of date. Filters cannot provide snooze, schedules, or replacement content, and they need manual repair, but no extension needs maintaining. If you want your own tool, a small unpacked build is reasonable and [needs only Developer mode](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/get-started/tutorial/hello-world), with manual updates. **For a public product, expect a hard sell:** you would face free, highly rated defaults, with no data on willingness to pay and no revenue figures for paid rivals such as UnTrap.

## Fork for speed, but license and architecture decide which

The license sets the route. **NFE is the best multi-site base and has the cleanest per-site selector data, but [v3.0.0 moved it from MIT to AGPL-3.0](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), and the [last MIT tag is MV2](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/blob/v2.3.3/src/manifest.json).** A fork therefore means publishing your source under AGPL or porting older code. The maintainer [explicitly welcomes forks](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator) for more complex versions, which reduces social friction. Legal review is worthwhile before any closed or paid use.

For YouTube alone, RYS is the better base: MPL-2.0, plain JavaScript, and [YouTube-only permissions](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/blob/main/src/chrome_manifest.json). Its [premium server](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/blob/main/server/README.md) and [analytics path](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/blob/main/src/shared/analytics.js) would need stripping first. Control Panel for Twitter is the MIT option for X, though at [8,284 lines in one script](https://github.com/insin/control-panel-for-twitter/blob/master/script.js) it is not light. Control Panel for YouTube and Antigram have public code but no detected license, so neither grants reuse by default. For a permissive multi-site product, write fresh, or first evaluate the small MIT projects [Escape the Algorithm](https://github.com/nestolobo/escapethealgorithm) and [Hushdoom](https://github.com/ixismail/hushdoom); their code was not inspected. A fork saves initial build effort but not maintenance, and it inherits no store listing, reviews, or users.

Three design choices would address the evidence directly. First, put default-on hiding in manifest-declared CSS, which Chrome injects [before any DOM is constructed](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/content-scripts), and let scripts turn hiding off. NFE instead [pings its service worker before writing styles](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/blob/main/src/entrypoints/intercept/intercept.tsx), the likely source of its flash bug, though no one has measured flash in any extension. [Declarative network redirects](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/declarativeNetRequest) work on network requests, so by inference they would miss in-app navigation and are at most a supplement.

Second, ship selector updates as remote data. Store reviews [usually take days and can take weeks](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/review-process), and [skip-review covers only declarative rule files](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/skip-review), not CSS or content scripts. Chrome's guidance says remote code ["does not include data or things like JSON or CSS"](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/remote-hosted-code), and policy allows remote configuration while prohibiting [interpreters that run fetched commands](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/mv3-requirements). A flat site-to-selectors file therefore reads as low risk, but that is an interpretation. Google has no statement on selector lists, no open-source feed blocker examined does this, and [researchers have documented abuse of remote config](https://palant.info/2025/01/20/malicious-extensions-circumvent-googles-remote-code-ban/), so enforcement could tighten. Keep a bundled fallback and disclose the fetch in the listing.

Third, detect failures automatically. RYS's breakages surfaced through a [signed-in Playwright suite](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/pull/242), and a nightly version makes reliability measurable. Measured over 12 months, churn was [28 selector commits at NFE](https://github.com/jordwest/news-feed-eradicator/commits/main/src/sitelist) (about 7 true repairs, by commit title) and [37 content-script commits at RYS](https://github.com/lawrencehook/remove-youtube-suggestions/commits/main/src/content-script) for YouTube alone, and both are upper bounds on breakage. Publishing is the small cost: [a $5 fee](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/whats-new), a listing, and a [single narrow purpose](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/policies). Request specific hosts or [optional per-site permissions](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/declare-permissions), and avoid bundling a dashboard or general site blocker, which risks a [second-purpose violation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/troubleshooting).

The evidence has limits. Chrome low-star review text could not be read in bulk, Reddit was inaccessible, so less technical users are underrepresented, and no extension was installed or tested. All surface lists are listing claims, and several prices are unverified.

## Conclusion

The incumbents' weakness is operational, not conceptual: hiding a feed is a solved, free problem, while keeping it hidden is an unfinished service. That shifts the question from "what feature is missing?" to "who will commit to the upkeep?" A new extension earns its place only if its maintainer does the unglamorous work indefinitely, which also makes it a poor fit for a one-off project unless you are its main user.

The cheapest useful test comes before any store listing: run signed-in nightly checks of existing blockers on LinkedIn, Facebook, and YouTube for a month. That would produce the break-frequency data no one has published, and it would show whether a real reliability gap exists on Chrome today. If it does, start with those sites. If contributing fixes and pressing for a faster NFE release would close most of the gap, that outperforms launching a fourth or fifth competitor.
